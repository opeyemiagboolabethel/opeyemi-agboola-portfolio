When Health Data Do Not Connect

Maternal Continuity of Care, Data Fragmentation and the Case for Interoperable Health Information Systems in Nigeria.

From analysis to policy

A separate policy output from the maternal-care project.

This brief draws on my survey-weighted maternal continuum-of-care analysis to examine the information systems needed to see and respond to gaps across pregnancy, delivery and early postnatal care.

It connects the analytical findings with data sharing, the health-data value chain and digital public infrastructure. The policy output complements the technical project rather than representing another independent dataset.

Project-derived estimates from the 2024 NDHS, as reported in the policy brief. The combined outcome requires four or more antenatal contacts, skilled birth attendance and early postnatal care for both mother and newborn. Separate service percentages are not a sequential dropout cohort.

The policy question

Can relevant information follow a woman as care moves across facilities?

Maternal care may cross a primary health centre, a laboratory, a referral hospital and a postnatal service. The brief considers how common definitions, patient identification and safe information exchange can support that journey.

Evidence boundary: the survey analysis does not establish that fragmented digital systems caused the observed service gaps. Interoperability is the policy proposition explored in the brief, not a tested causal explanation.

Priority recommendations

Five practical directions for connected maternal-health information.

01 / Use case

Make maternal continuity an interoperability priority.

Use the real care pathway to test whether information can move meaningfully across antenatal care, referral, delivery and follow-up.

02 / Common meaning

Define a minimum interoperable dataset.

Agree essential maternal and newborn data elements, identifiers, terminology and exchange rules across participating systems.

03 / Linkage

Strengthen identification and referral linkage.

Connect encounters through shared registries and referral information so continuity can be understood across service points.

04 / Governance

Build trust into data sharing.

Clarify lawful purpose, minimum necessary access, role-based permissions, authentication, audit trails, security and institutional accountability.

05 / Decision intelligence

Monitor continuity as well as service counts.

Use linked pathway and equity views to support review of referral completion, missed transitions and differences across communities.
