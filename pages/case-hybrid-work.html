Research objective

The question behind the analysis

When hybrid-work policy clarity and HR support are considered simultaneously, which remains more strongly associated with employee job satisfaction?

This case study presents the study design, statistical approach and findings in a clear, independently reviewable format. Methodological qualifications are stated alongside the results.

What the evidence suggests
Support provided by human resources showed a stronger conditional association with job satisfaction than policy clarity.

Policy communication may matter, but the adjusted model did not establish its independent contribution.

Methodology

How the research was carried out.

  1. Descriptive cross-sectional research design, with a 384-person structured questionnaire
  2. Three ten-item five-point Likert scales measuring policy clarity, HR support and employee satisfaction
  3. Internal-consistency checks using Cronbach's alpha (0.879, 0.899 and 0.908)
  4. Pearson correlations and two-predictor multiple linear regression, with coefficient-level interpretation
Interpretation and limitations. Reported findings from an existing cross-sectional study. Correlation and regression do not establish that workplace policies or HR support caused satisfaction. Original respondent records and identifiable thesis files are not distributed.
