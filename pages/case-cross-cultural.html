Research objective

The question behind the analysis

How were cross-cultural recruitment strategies and multicultural collaboration related to perceived team productivity?

This case study presents the study design, statistical approach and findings in a clear, independently reviewable format. Methodological qualifications are stated alongside the results.

What the evidence suggests
Respondents reporting stronger multicultural collaboration also tended to report higher team productivity.

Associations were positive for both recruitment and collaboration; the survey did not establish causal effects.

Methodology

How the research was carried out.

  1. Structured cross-sectional survey with 422 valid responses
  2. Ten-item measures for cross-cultural recruitment, multicultural collaboration and team productivity
  3. Cronbach's alpha internal-consistency assessment (0.858, 0.873 and 0.897)
  4. Descriptive tables and Pearson correlations for the two research hypotheses
Interpretation and limitations. This was survey-based observational research using perceived productivity measures. High correlations do not establish a causal recruitment or teamwork effect. No multivariable regression estimates are claimed here.
