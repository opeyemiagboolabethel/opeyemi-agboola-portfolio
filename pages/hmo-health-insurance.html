The Health Insurance Gap in Nigeria

Understanding the decisions behind insurance uptake: affordability, trust, perceived value and the experience of accessing care.

NorthStar Insights · 202415 participantsSemi-structured interviewsThematic analysis
Read the report ↗Download report ↓

The question

Why can insurance remain unappealing even when people see the need for healthcare?

This research work at NorthStar Insights explored how people understand health insurance, what they expect it to cover and what makes participation feel worthwhile or difficult.

The report brings together consumer and provider perspectives. It treats insurance decisions as a question of affordability, credibility and usable access, rather than assuming price alone explains uptake.

Attribution: the original report is credited to NorthStar. It is presented here as work from my research experience at NorthStar Insights, not as a sole-authored independent publication.

Research design

Listening for the reasons behind the decision.

The study used purposive sampling and semi-structured interviews with 15 participants from varied occupational, income and educational backgrounds. Interview material was coded and grouped through thematic analysis.

The report describes informed consent and the use of changed names and removed identifying details. This portfolio page shares themes and the supplied report, not raw interview records.

Interpretation: this is a small exploratory qualitative study. Its themes should not be read as representative national prevalence estimates or causal effects.

Themes in the report

The gap between an insurance product and a credible promise of care.

01 / Affordability

Premiums compete with immediate needs.

Participants described irregular earnings and competing household expenses. These accounts suggest the importance of matching payment structures to how prospective users actually earn and spend.

02 / Trust & value

Being covered must mean something in practice.

Accounts of exclusions, additional payments and provider acceptance raised concerns about whether a plan would deliver when needed. Clear benefits and a credible service experience matter alongside awareness.

03 / Access

A plan is less useful if care feels out of reach.

The report highlights perceptions of facility quality, travel and provider networks. These themes connect insurance product design with the realities of accessing healthcare.

04 / Understanding & familiarity

People assess insurance within existing care habits.

Unclear coverage information and established preferences for managing illness affected how participants viewed insurance. Communication needs to address questions and lived context rather than assume information alone changes behaviour.

Decision relevance

From research themes to questions insurers can act on.

Can payment options accommodate irregular income? Are exclusions understood before enrolment? Is the provider network useful to the intended audience? Are complaints and unexpected costs informing service improvement?

These are research-informed service-design questions. The study does not establish that its recommendations were implemented or produced measurable uptake improvements.
